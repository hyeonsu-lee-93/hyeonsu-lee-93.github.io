Newborn animals can compare visual quantities before any visual experience. Where does such an ability come from?
Using biologically inspired model networks, I showed that single units selective to the proportion and the difference
between two visual quantities emerge in randomly initialized feedforward circuits, and that these units are sufficient for
the network to perform quantity comparison. A random summation of monotonic, nonlinear responses explains why two distinct
kinds of tuning appear. The same approach extends to other innate functions, such as aesthetic preferences.
